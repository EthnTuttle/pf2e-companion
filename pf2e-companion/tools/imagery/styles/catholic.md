# Style block — catholic (illumination / stained glass / altarpiece)

Register (grounded in `catholic/pack.toml`: "closest in tone to Tolkien's
Silmarillion and a medieval-Catholic vibe"; nine-choir Pseudo-Dionysian
hierarchy): the trecento-to-baroque devotional register — gold-ground
illumination marginalia for small slots, stained-glass leaded color for
saints, chiaroscuro altarpiece drama for vistas.

Palette (named colors): deep ultramarine, gold leaf, crimson lake, bone
parchment, emerald; Marian blue reserved for `mary-queen-of-heaven`.

Composition: frontal iconic saint with attribute and thin gold halo; arched
frame *within* the image (the style's own architecture, never a drawn literal
border); vistas as apse-scale heavenly drama (purgatory as luminous ascent,
never horror).

## Prefix

Devotional panel painting in the late-medieval-to-baroque Catholic register:
leaded stained-glass color with egg-tempera and gold-leaf luminosity, deep
ultramarine and crimson and gold on bone parchment, solemn frontal hieratic
composition, each holy figure identified by a traditional attribute, thin gold
halo, drapery in deep folds. Never depict God the Father as a person. Subject:

## Suffix

Reverent devotional-fantasy illustration, dignified, no gore. Absolutely no
text, no letters, no inscriptions (any halo lettering must be abstract
glyph-marks only), no watermark, no border, no literal frame edge, no logo.
No copyrighted fantasy-franchise visual references. No real identifiable
living or recent persons. Never depict God the Father as a person (use the
hand of God, the Chi-Rho form, the Lamb, or the dove instead); never the
Trinity as three men.

## Do-Not Checklist (curation gate)

- NO depiction of God the Father as a person (no "Ancient of Days" old man;
  hand / Lamb / Chi-Rho / dove only); NO Trinity-as-three-men.
- NO readable text, letters, inscriptions, watermark, border, frame edge, logo
  (abstract halo glyph-marks tolerable; review by hand).
- NO horror-gore hell, NO irreverent contemporary dress, NO clerical satire,
  NO televangelist kitsch, nothing sexualized.
- Marian blue ONLY on `mary-queen-of-heaven`.
- Christ may be depicted (the Incarnation is the warrant); Mary with
  hyperdulia restraint; saints with their traditional attribute.
- Would a thoughtful Catholic recognize this as affectionate rather than
  kitsch?

## Calibration Decisions

- DEDUCTION §5.3(3c): saints render in the **stained-glass (leaded color)**
  register, not photo-baroque. Rationale: reads cleanly at portrait sizes,
  more forgiving of model drift in hands/drapery, and stylization keeps the
  devotional register honest rather than pastiche. Vistas may lean baroque
  altarpiece chiaroscuro; the "no literal frame edge" negative still binds.
