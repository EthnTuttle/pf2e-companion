# Style block — lewisian (storybook ink-and-wash plate)

Register (grounded in `lewisian/pack.toml` and both adventures' "Mere-Christianity
posture" notes): the mid-century British children's-book plate — Pauline Baynes
for Narnia, early Tolkien illustration. Pen-and-ink linework with translucent
watercolor wash; visible paper texture; intimate readerly scale.

Palette (named colors): warm cream paper, iron-gall ink brown-black, lamp-lit
amber, moss green, slate grey-blue rain, oxblood red accents, and exactly one
small point of pale-gold light per image (the Lewisian "Light" leitmotif).

Composition: vignette with natural white margin falloff; figures small within
landscape/interior; weather and light doing emotional work.

## Prefix

Vintage mid-century British storybook illustration plate, ink and watercolor on
warm cream paper, fine pen linework with translucent washes, gentle vignette
edges fading to paper, intimate cozy scale, lamp-lit amber and moss-green and
slate-grey palette with a single small pale-gold light source. Subject (never
a frontal portrait of any divine person):

## Suffix

 Reverent devotional-fantasy illustration in the manner of a classic
children's-book plate, dignified, no gore, nothing cutesy. Absolutely no text,
no letters, no inscriptions, no legible signage (any writing must be illegible
abstract pen-strokes), no watermark, no border, no frame, no logo. No
copyrighted fantasy-franchise visual references. No real identifiable living
or recent persons. Never depict any Person of the Godhead frontally: any
teacher-figure appears only from behind, at distance, mid-gesture, or implied
by light and crowd reaction.

## Do-Not Checklist (curation gate)

- NO frontal depiction of any Person of the Godhead (Teacher-figure: rear /
  distant / mid-gesture / light-and-crowd only).
- NO readable text, letters, inscriptions, signage, watermark, border, or
  frame edge.
- NO denominational vestments or mock medievalism.
- NO cutesy/chibi register, NO gore, NO modern photo-realism.
- Exactly one small pale-gold light point; the image must not become a
  "golden glow everywhere" poster.
- Would a thoughtful reader of Lewis recognize this as affectionate rather
  than kitsch?

## Calibration Decisions

- Covers and vistas are scene plates, not portraits — consistent with §3.1
  "scenes, places, ordinary people, symbolic objects".
- The Teacher (roof-of-mercy) is token-rendered rear/distant per §3.1; no v1
  asset tests the frontal-divine boundary.
